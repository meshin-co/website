## What goes into each cost?

Start with the work you need the officer to own. Include decisions, staff support and any work needed for customer reviews. Then ask each candidate to describe the capacity needed to cover it.

| Cost or commitment    | Fractional officer                              | Full-time hire                                          |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Direct cost           | Agreed fee and any separately scoped work       | Salary, benefits and any equity                         |
| Getting started       | Time spent agreeing scope and sharing context   | Recruiting, selection and ramp time                     |
| Coverage              | Availability set in the agreement               | Dedicated capacity, subject to competing duties         |
| Work outside the role | Staff or other providers may need to cover gaps | Specialist work may still need other staff or providers |
| Change or exit        | Handover and any agreed exit terms              | Hiring a successor and transferring the role            |

Benefits belong in the comparison alongside salary. The [Bureau of Labor Statistics data on employer costs](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) separates wages and salaries from benefits. Use your own proposed terms and employment costs rather than treating a market average as a quote.

## How much coverage are you buying?

A fractional fee should state when the officer is available and what work it covers. Ask how urgent requests are handled and who can make a decision when the officer is away. An internal owner still needs time to carry tasks between reviews.

A full-time officer has dedicated capacity, but that capacity can be consumed by work outside AI. Set priorities and decision rights for the role. The [Bureau of Labor Statistics description of executive work](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/top-executives.htm) gives context for the breadth of an executive role.

Compare both options against the same [AI operating model](/glossary/ai-operating-model). Record who sets direction, who delivers the work and who can approve a change. The [fractional officer FAQ](/faq/why-hire-a-fractional-officer-instead-of-a-full-time-head-of-ai) explains the case for a scoped leadership role.

## Which arrangement fits the work?

Choose a scoped engagement when leadership decisions arise intermittently and an internal owner can follow through. Put the review rhythm and the work left with staff in writing. Ask what happens if demand grows beyond the agreed scope.

A full-time role deserves consideration when teams need daily direction and the work can sustain a dedicated post. Test that assumption with a list of decisions and duties, not just a job title. Either option may need support from legal, security or engineering staff.

The contract matters as well as the title. The [IRS guidance on independent contractors](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-defined) explains how control over work affects classification. Take advice on the proposed arrangement rather than assuming a fractional title settles it.

## What should you agree before signing?

Ask for a written scope covering outputs, access to the officer and the decisions they can make. Name the person who owns tasks between meetings. Set a review point for changing the scope if demand shifts.

Agree how records and open decisions will transfer when the engagement ends. The [handover playbook](/resources/handover-playbook) can help structure that transfer.
