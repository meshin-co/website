shortDefinition: A high-risk AI system is one the EU AI Act classifies as carrying its heaviest legal duties. That happens either because it is a safety component of a regulated product, or because it serves a listed purpose such as hiring, credit scoring or law enforcement. A narrow exemption can apply.
definition: |-
  A system reaches the category one of two ways. Article 6(1) covers the first: a safety component of, or itself, a product already regulated under EU product-safety law, such as medical devices or machinery. That underlying law already requires third-party conformity assessment.

  Annex III covers the second: eight listed use-case areas, among them biometric identification, employment decisions, credit scoring and law enforcement. Article 6(2) makes a system high-risk for landing in one of those areas alone, with no product-safety law involved at all.

  A system inside Annex III can still escape the label under Article 6(3), for four narrow reasons. One is a limited procedural task: reformatting an application into a standard template, say. Another is improving a human decision already made, such as tidying a report's wording without changing its recommendation.

  A third is detecting a deviation without replacing human judgement, such as flagging an unusual transaction for a fraud analyst to review. The fourth is a preparatory task ahead of someone else's assessment, such as gathering a loan applicant's documents before a human underwriter looks at them. None of the four survives contact with profiling: a system that profiles individual people is high-risk regardless.

## Why it comes up

It is the first question every other AI Act obligation depends on. A customer's legal team or an investor's due diligence checklist asks it before anything else about the system. A board hears it phrased more simply, as "are we in the category that gets the heavy paperwork, and who decided." Getting the classification wrong either way has a real cost, either missed obligations or needless process on a system that never required it.

## What to do about it

Run the classification test on each system separately, not on the company as a whole. One product can be high-risk while another, built the same month, is not. Write the decision down, including the reasoning for a not-high-risk conclusion, since an authority can ask to see it later.
