shortDefinition: The EU AI Act is an EU regulation, in force since August 2024, that sorts AI systems into risk tiers, from banned practices to high-risk systems, and sets legal obligations that scale with the tier.
definition: The EU AI Act, formally Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, is an EU regulation that entered into force on 1 August 2024. It sorts AI systems by risk, running from a short list of banned practices, through a "high-risk" category covering things like AI used in hiring, credit or law enforcement, a lighter set of transparency duties for systems like chatbots, and everything else left largely unregulated. Obligations scale with the tier, and high-risk systems carry duties such as a conformity assessment, technical documentation, human oversight and registration in an EU database. It applies to any company whose AI system is used in the EU, not only companies based there, which is why it comes up even for a US-based startup with European customers. In practice, it changes what "we did not think about that" costs. For a high-risk use case, a company now needs a documented risk assessment, a named point of human oversight, and evidence of both, before a regulator or a customer's legal team ever asks.

## Why it comes up

It comes up the moment a company has, or wants, a customer or user in the EU, regardless of where the company itself is based. A customer's legal team asks which risk tier your product falls into before they will sign, because their own exposure depends on your answer. A board asks about it once a competitor gets fined or a customer raises it in a renewal conversation. Founders who assume it is "an EU problem" for EU companies are usually surprised to learn the Act follows the AI system's users, not the provider's registered address.

## What to do about it

Work out which risk tier each of your AI systems actually falls into. Most products used inside a normal business are not high-risk, but a few categories, hiring and credit among them, almost always are, and deserve a real legal read rather than a guess. For anything in or near the high-risk category, start the paperwork now: a risk assessment, a named human oversight point, and technical documentation, because these take longer to produce honestly than to promise. For everything else, keep the register entry current so the answer is a lookup, not a scramble.
