definition: The NIST AI Risk Management Framework, usually shortened to the AI RMF, is a voluntary framework published by the US National Institute of Standards and Technology in January 2023. It sets out four functions an organisation applies to an AI system, GOVERN for policy and accountability, MAP for understanding the context and the risk, MEASURE for testing and metrics, and MANAGE for acting on what you found. It is not a law and it is not sector specific, so a bank, a hospital and a ten-person startup can all use the same structure. US procurement teams and increasingly non-US ones ask about it because it gives a common vocabulary for a question that used to have no fixed answer, namely how you know your AI system's risks are actually being managed, rather than just discussed once. In practice, adopting it means someone can point to a specific function and a specific piece of evidence for it.

## Why it comes up

It comes up in almost every vendor security questionnaire and in board packs once a company has more than one AI system running. A customer's risk team wants a framework they recognise, not a bespoke description of your process, because it lets them compare you against every other vendor they review. A board wants the same thing for a different reason: it gives them a structure to ask questions against, instead of having to trust that engineering has thought of everything. Because it is US government work and free to read, it has become the default reference even for companies outside the US.

## What to do about it

Read the four functions once, properly, rather than skimming a summary. Then map what you already do against GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE and MANAGE, and write down the gaps honestly rather than the parts you already cover. You do not need to implement all of it before you can talk about it. A short document that says "here is our AI register, here is our review step, here is what we still owe you" against each function is more convincing in a review than a claim of full compliance with nothing behind it.
