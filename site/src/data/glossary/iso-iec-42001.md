definition: ISO/IEC 42001 is an international standard published in December 2023 by ISO/IEC's joint technical committee for artificial intelligence. It specifies requirements for an AI management system, the recurring set of policies, roles and processes an organisation runs to govern how it develops, provides or uses AI, using the same Plan-Do-Check-Act structure as other ISO management system standards such as ISO/IEC 27001 for information security. Unlike the NIST AI RMF, which is a free framework, ISO/IEC 42001 is a standard a company can be audited and certified against by an accredited third party. Enterprise customers and procurement teams ask for it, or ask where you sit against it, because certification gives them a documented, externally checked answer instead of a vendor's own claim. In practice, working towards it means writing down a risk assessment method, running an AI system inventory, and reviewing that inventory on a schedule, whether or not a company pursues certification itself.

## Why it comes up

A procurement team building a vendor shortlist asks for it by name once they have seen it on a competitor's security page. A board that already understands ISO 27001 from an information security audit reaches for the same mental model here, and asks whether an equivalent exists for AI. Because it is a certifiable standard rather than a public framework, it carries more weight in a formal audit than a self-written policy, which is exactly why a customer's legal team may put it on a checklist even before they have read what is actually in it.

## What to do about it

Full certification is a real project with an external auditor, and most companies at seed to growth stage do not need it yet. What is worth doing now is running the standard's own structure without the audit: a documented AI risk assessment method, an inventory of the AI systems in operation, and a review of that inventory at a fixed interval. That gives you an honest answer of "not certified, but built to the same structure" for a customer who asks, which is a stronger position than either silence or an unsupported claim.
